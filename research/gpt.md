# Adult psychiatric emergency risk factors: research register

Research date: 2026-11-07. Intended use: clinician teaching. **This evidence does not support a checkbox calculator that multiplies factors into a patient-specific risk.** `null` means a defensible figure for the requested application was not verified, not that the factor is irrelevant.

## Interpretation and comparison-group legend

- GP-unexposed = people without that exposure in general-population source studies, not the average population including exposed people. PA controls = nonsuicide controls in general-population psychological-autopsy studies (living or other-death controls, depending on study); ascertainment and matching vary. Psychosis-unexposed = other people with psychosis without that factor: explicitly a clinical comparator.
- Preserve OR, RR, HR and SMR labels. They are not interchangeable. Pooled estimates generally have no single prediction horizon; historical diagnoses do not quantify imminent danger.
- Violence means perpetration unless **victimization** is specified. Homicide victimization cannot estimate a patient's probability of committing homicide. Attempt, suicide death, self-harm and composite suicidal behavior must remain distinct.
- Source rows were checked using abstracts, open text or tables. This is targeted literature research, not a systematic review. Null rows cite a relevant framework or contextual study, not a claimed quantitative result. Chronic/acute assignment is an indexing choice: history and recent events overlap.

### Psychiatric discharge: report rates instead of inventing an RR

Chung et al. found **2,950 suicide deaths per 100,000 person-years** during the **first week** after psychiatric hospitalization (95% CI **1,740–5,000**) and **2,060 per 100,000 person-years** during the **first month** (95% CI **1,300–3,280**). These are annualized incidence rates within short windows, **not** 2.95% in a week or 2.06% in a month. Heterogeneity and publication bias were substantial. [Chung et al., 2019, BMJ Open, DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2018-023883; PMID 30904843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30904843/).

The earlier synthesis reported **1,132 per 100,000 person-years** within three months (95% CI **874–1,467**). It is a different pooled window and dataset, not a coefficient to multiply by the first-week rate. [Chung et al., 2017, JAMA Psychiatry, PMID 28564699](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28564699/).

Do not divide these pooled international rates by the current US age-adjusted suicide rate and label the quotient an empirically estimated patient RR. The age mix, country, calendar period, ascertainment and standardization differ. A teaching-only conversion of an incidence rate to a probability requires a stated constant-hazard assumption over the specified window; risk after discharge is time-varying.

## Important evidence qualifications

**Adult safe storage:** the two Shenassa estimates are verified, but their comparison is firearm-suicide decedents versus natural-death decedents. A 2025 reanalysis found no clear association between locking/unloading and firearm versus nonfirearm suicide among adult male suicide decedents and explains why the older estimates should not be interpreted as a general adult suicide reduction. This does not establish that storage is useless or overturn the broader rationale for reducing actual access during crisis. Owners may retain keys/combinations. For this adult app, keep the classic figures as study-specific evidence, not a default protective multiplier. [Miller et al., 2025, “Firearm Storage and Firearm Suicide,” JAMA Network Open](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12235496/).

**Campbell model selection:** the firearm-access OR changes as covariates enter: model 2 is 7.59, whereas model 6 is 5.38; the firearm effect is no longer significant after the incident's actual gun use enters model 7. The table intentionally uses model 2 because its CI was verified. The separation estimate is model 3. These are associations among already abused women, not general-population homicide RRs. Do not combine their coefficients across model stages. Strangulation figures come from **Glass 2008**, not an independent significant final-model Campbell coefficient. [Campbell et al., 2003, full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1447915/).

**Diagnostic estimates:** methods matter. Psychological-autopsy ORs, register-based mortality ratios and treatment trials address different questions. Psychiatric emergency populations are selected on many of these exposures. An association against general-population controls can shrink markedly within a clinical sample. Diagnosis alone is insufficient for imminent-risk formulation; most people with a psychiatric diagnosis do not perpetrate serious violence.

**Null entries are intentional:** there are studies on hopelessness, chronic pain, sexual-minority attempt disparities, psychopathy, command hallucinations and recent release. A missing universal general-population coefficient does not mean there is no literature. For example, the schizophrenia-specific review reports hopelessness OR **4.64 (2.24–9.63)** for suicide, comparing patients with schizophrenia; this should not replace a general-population hopelessness multiplier. [Cassidy et al., 2018, Schizophrenia Bulletin, full text/table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6007264/).

## Combining factors: what is and is not defensible

1. **Do not multiply the table's point estimates.** Depression, hopelessness, insomnia, attempts, substance use, hospitalization and firearm access correlate; some lie on the same causal pathway. Marginal associations from separate studies double-count shared information. Some rows describe the same exposure at different thresholds or outcomes. Interactions, treatment selection and confounding further break a simple product model. Independence is not established by statistical significance. Conversely, dependence does not mathematically guarantee overstatement in every case; the practical problem is that the product is unvalidated and often inflates risk.
2. **A relative association is not a predictive test.** Franklin et al. reviewed **365 longitudinal studies / 3,428 effects** and found prediction only slightly better than chance across outcomes, no broad factor category performing well, no improvement over 50 years, and little examination of combinations. Their findings concern the studied predictors and methods, not proof that all future prediction is impossible. [Franklin et al., 2017, Psychol Bull, DOI 10.1037/bul0000084; PMID 27841450](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27841450/).
3. **High/low categories leave many deaths outside “high risk.”** Large et al.'s psychiatric cohort meta-analysis found high versus lower risk OR **4.84 (3.79–6.20)**, sensitivity about **56%** and specificity about **79%**. Around 44% of suicides were outside the high-risk category; categories are not reliable disposition rules. A related 2017 scales review estimated suicide PPV **5.5% (3.9–7.9)**, with heterogeneous settings and horizons, not imminent ED risk. [Large et al., 2016, PLoS ONE, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0156322](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27285387/); [Large et al., 2017, BJPsych Bulletin, uncertainty discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5451650/); [Carter et al., 2017, Br J Psychiatry, PMID 28302700](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28302700/).
4. **Use joint models only in their intended population, horizon and version.** A multivariable model fits conditional coefficients together and estimates its own baseline risk. Its exact coefficients, intercept/baseline survival, coding, handling of missingness and calibration are required. Do not substitute pooled ORs from this register into a published model.
5. **Even single-factor absolute conversion needs the right reference.** For a genuine OR and reference probability `p0` for the same outcome/window/population: `p1 = OR*p0/(1-p0+OR*p0)`. For an HR under proportional hazards: `p1(t) = 1 - S0(t)^HR`. For an RR: `p1 = RR*p0` only if its reference and horizon match. These algebraic identities do not validate an arbitrary US population baseline for a selected ED patient.

**Suggested app behavior:** display each selected factor's source-specific association, confidence interval, comparator and outcome; label clinical comparators prominently. Present a narrative formulation of baseline vulnerability, recent change, plausible scenarios, means access and actions. If a numerical combination is desired for teaching, use a complete validated model in a clearly eligible hypothetical case, with its stated timeframe, or label the combined probability **not estimable from these factors**. Do not emit a clinician-facing pseudo-precise risk number from arbitrary products.

## Key clinical frameworks to cite in the app

- **SAFE-T:** identify risk factors, identify protective factors, inquire about suicide, determine intervention/triage, and document rationale/follow-up. Reassess after clinical changes and at discharge. Protective factors do not necessarily offset major acute risk. [SAMHSA, SAFE-T 2024](https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/safet-flyer-pep24-01-036.pdf).
- **C-SSRS:** distinguish wish to die, active thoughts, method, intent, plan, preparatory behavior and actual/interrupted/aborted attempts. [Columbia official resource](https://www.columbiapsychiatry.org/research-labs/columbia-suicide-severity-rating-scale-c-ssrs); [Posner et al., 2011 validation, PMID 22193671](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22193671/).
- **HCR-20 V3:** trained structured professional judgment and violence management, including relevant history, current symptoms/intent and future circumstances. Use the manual and appropriate training; do not present a homemade checklist as the licensed instrument. [Douglas et al., 2013, official author resource](https://www.sfu.ca/psychology/research/drl/hcr-20--version-3-/).
- **Joint Commission NPSG 15.01.01:** environmental safety, validated screening, evidence-based assessment following positive screens, documented overall risk/mitigation, policies, discharge counseling/follow-up, and monitoring implementation. Applicability depends on accreditation program and care setting. The 2026 Behavioral Health Care/Human Services requirements retain this identifier; verify the applicable hospital-program standards before claiming compliance. [Joint Commission resource center](https://www.jointcommission.org/en-us/knowledge-library/suicide-prevention); [2026 BHC requirements](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/495eaf1731824b748fe5a3384da1da99?v=5e625246).
- **Stanley–Brown Safety Planning Intervention:** collaborative warning signs, internal coping, social distraction, supports, professional/crisis contacts and means safety; follow-up and revision matter. The ED study in the table tested SPI **plus follow-up**, so its effect should not be attributed to a completed form alone. [Stanley et al., 2018, PMID 29998307](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29998307/).
- **CALM:** Counseling on Access to Lethal Means teaches identifying people who benefit, asking about actual access, and collaborative reduction of access with patients/families. It is a counseling approach, not an individual RR. [SPRC official CALM resource](https://sprc.org/resources/calm-counseling-on-access-to-lethal-means/).

## Ten numerical figures requiring the most caution before app use

This ranks uncertainty in **interpretation/transferability**, not known transcription errors. All listed estimates were located in cited sources. Null rows are not silently assigned surrogate numbers.

1. **Locked storage OR 0.39:** selected decedent controls; disputed adult causal interpretation in the 2025 reanalysis. Do not use as automatic 61% protection.
2. **Unloaded storage OR 0.30:** same problem; not independent of locking or actual access; not all-method mortality.
3. **Lithium OR 0.13:** classic estimate with sparse events; newer RCT synthesis OR 0.30 has CI crossing 1. Not a settled universal treatment multiplier.
4. **High acute alcohol OR 37.18:** wide CI, four studies, heterogeneous high-dose definitions; attempt outcome, not death.
5. **Psychosis/homicide OR 19.5:** historical samples and legal systems, heterogeneous diagnoses and comorbidity; rare perpetration outcome; poor imminent-risk transfer.
6. **Campbell firearm OR 7.59:** intermediate model 2, abused-woman control group, proxy interviews; differs from adjusted later-model 5.38. No general-population interpretation.
7. **Glass strangulation OR 7.48:** IPV case-control selection and retrospective history; homicide victimization, not a general strangulation-to-homicide absolute probability.
8. **Religious attendance HR 0.16:** female nurse cohort, low event count and observational confounding; cannot generalize to all religions, adults or emergency patients.
9. **Poor impulse-control OR 3.3:** converted continuous-scale effect, older mixed designs, variable measures. A binary checkbox is not its original exposure.
10. **ECA annual violence 3.7%:** verified via a published review table rather than the accessible original abstract; three historical sites and broad behavior definition. Weak choice for a current national baseline.

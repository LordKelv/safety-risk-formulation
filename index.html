Safety Risk Formulation: Harm to Self & Others

Teaching tool · not a prediction instrument
How to read this. Each factor shows its published association with suicide or violence, the comparison group the figure came from, and its reference. Figures marked vs general population are compared with people without that factor in the general population; vs clinical / subgroup figures come from within already-selected groups (e.g., people with psychosis, abused women) and are not population multipliers. Risk factors predict suicide and violence only slightly better than chance (Franklin et al., 2017), and categorizing patients as "high" or "low" risk misses many people who go on to die (Large et al., 2016). Use this tool to structure a formulation (baseline vulnerability, acute change, access to means, protective factors, plan), never to decide a disposition by a number. Nothing you select is stored or transmitted.

    How the illustrative combined estimate is calculated

    Multiplying relative risks assumes each factor is independent. They are not: depression, hopelessness, insomnia, substance use and prior attempts cluster in the same people, so a naive product badly overstates risk. This tool therefore shows a range rather than a point:

    • Lower end: the single strongest selected factor alone (as if every other factor were fully redundant).
    • Diamond (central): factors sorted by strength, each added on the log scale at a geometrically decreasing weight (1, ½, ¼, …), so additional correlated factors add progressively less.
    • Upper end: the naive product (as if fully independent), shown only to illustrate how much independence assumptions inflate risk. Values are capped at 500×.
    • Protective factors are discounted the same way and then applied at half weight, reflecting SAFE-T guidance that protective factors do not negate acute risk.
    • Demographic baseline (sex) is a rate ratio to the overall population and is applied directly.
    • Only factors with general-population comparators are included unless you tick the box above. Factors without a usable figure (e.g., hopelessness, plan with intent, threats) are listed separately and do not lower the number, even though they may matter most clinically.

    This damping scheme is a teaching heuristic, not a validated model. Validated multivariable tools exist for narrow populations: OxMIS for suicide in severe mental illness, OxRec for violent reoffending after prison. They cannot be reproduced from pooled odds ratios.
